## Critical thinking and verification

Children should practise asking what evidence supports a claim and what might be missing. Give two different answers to the same question and ask which can be checked. Make it normal to revise a view when new information appears.

## Communication and collaboration

A useful task asks a team to design a low-cost community service. Each child contributes an idea, listens to another and explains a decision. Allow spoken, typed, drawn and AAC contributions.

## Creativity with constraints

Ask children to solve a real-world problem using only three available materials or a fictional £20 budget. Constraints make them compare options and explain trade-offs rather than produce endless ideas.

## Digital and AI literacy

Teach that AI systems can generate text and images but may make mistakes, reflect biases or mishandle personal information. Use adult-led demonstrations and fictional prompts. Children should not be required to create independent accounts on general-purpose AI services.

## Self-regulation and reflection

Teach a simple cycle: plan, try, check, improve. Ask what worked and what to change next time. Explicit modelling and guided reflection have a stronger evidence base than assuming these skills develop automatically from screen time.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
