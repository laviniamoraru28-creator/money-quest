> Use small problem-solving projects to teach empathy, planning and responsible experimentation.

## Find a problem before inventing a product

Ask children to notice a small inconvenience, such as messy classroom supplies. Who experiences it? What would help? Invite several possible solutions before selecting one.

## Build a low-cost prototype

Use paper, recycled materials or drawings. Set a fictional budget and time limit. Children should test whether their idea helps the intended user, not simply whether it looks attractive.

## Understand costs and value

If materials cost £3 and the pretend selling price is £5, discuss the £2 difference before other costs. Explain that sales are uncertain and that real businesses also have time, transport and other expenses.

## Practise ethical choices

Ask whether an advertisement makes a promise the product cannot keep, whether everyone can access the solution and what happens to unused materials. Entrepreneurship includes responsibility to customers and communities.

## Reflect and improve

After feedback, ask what the child would keep, change or stop. Success means identifying a need, testing an idea and explaining a revision, not making money.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
