> Adapt money lessons for different communication, sensory, attention and learning needs without assuming one method suits every child.

## Begin with an individual access check

Ask whether the child understands pictures, spoken words, symbols, objects or written text most reliably. Check whether they can use a mouse, touch screen, switch or keyboard. Identify sensory barriers such as loud sounds, flashing rewards or crowded screens before choosing an activity.

## Teach one decision at a time

For example, show two pictured items and a visible £5 budget. Ask the child to choose an item that fits. Then add a third choice. Keep the task predictable and provide a clear 'finished' signal. Repeat with new items to see whether the concept transfers.

## Use AAC and alternative responses

A learner may select 'save', 'spend', 'need' or 'help' using an AAC system. Another may point to a symbol or move a coin. Record the decision and reason when possible, rather than scoring speech or writing ability.

## Make feedback calm and specific

Instead of a loud celebration for every tap, use a short message: 'You chose the £3 item. £2 remains.' Provide a retry without punishment. Let learners disable animation and narration independently.

## Check understanding beyond the game

Try the same decision with physical tokens or a new set of pictures. If performance changes, investigate whether the barrier is the concept, the interface or the language. SEND is diverse, and an adaptation that helps one learner may not help another.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
