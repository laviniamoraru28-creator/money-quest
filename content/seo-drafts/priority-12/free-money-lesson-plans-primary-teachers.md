> Three adaptable lesson plans with learning objectives, materials and checks for understanding.

## Lesson 1: Recognise ways to pay, ages 6 to 8

Objective: identify coins, notes and the idea of card or digital payment. Materials: printed images and pretend coins. Model a purchase, ask pupils to sort pictures, then check whether they can identify a payment method in a new picture. Do not use real cards or account information.

## Lesson 2: Plan within a budget, ages 9 to 11

Objective: compare options and explain a trade-off. Materials: fictional price cards and a £15 budget. Model your reasoning aloud. Groups plan a purchase, respond to a price change and explain the revised plan. Assess the explanation, not only the arithmetic.

## Lesson 3: Detect a risky offer, ages 11 to 14

Objective: identify urgency, missing evidence and requests for personal data. Materials: two fictional advertisements and a fake message. Pupils highlight warning signs and propose a safe next step. Explain that a suspicious message should be checked with a trusted adult.

## Adaptation and assessment

Offer symbol cards, spoken instructions, a quiet mode and alternative ways to respond. Use a short exit question with a new example. Collect only the learning response needed; avoid personal financial disclosures.

## Link to the wider curriculum

Connect budgeting to mathematics, advertisements to media literacy and choices to personal development. Keep each lesson focused on one objective, with practice before assessment.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
