# How to Teach Kids to Save Money at Different Ages

> Build saving habits through visible goals, realistic timeframes and choices that respect different family circumstances.

## Ages 6 to 8: make progress visible

Use a drawing of a jar and a fictional goal of £6. Add a £1 paper coin each time the character saves. Let the child count how much is left. Avoid promising that saving always happens at the same speed.

## Ages 9 to 11: introduce trade-offs

Give a fictional weekly amount of £4 and a £12 goal. Compare saving £2 per week with saving £3. Ask how the plan changes if an unexpected cost appears. A good answer can include changing the goal.

## Ages 12 to 14: compare options and risk

Discuss why savings are different from investments, how inflation affects purchasing power and why interest calculations depend on rates and time. Use hypothetical figures and explain that actual financial products have rules and risks.

## Keep family finances private

Children can learn with imaginary budgets. Do not require disclosure of allowance, income or bank balances. If a family cannot set aside money, planning and prioritising with tokens still teaches the underlying skill.

## Review without pressure

Ask once a week: 'What is the goal?', 'What changed?', 'What will we try next?' Reward the explanation and planning, not the amount saved.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
