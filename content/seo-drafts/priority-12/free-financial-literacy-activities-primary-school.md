> Six low-cost classroom and home activities using paper, pictures and imaginary money.

## The £10 shopping challenge

Give pairs of pupils a fictional £10 budget and picture cards showing a sandwich (£3), fruit (£2), a drink (£2) and a toy (£6). Ask them to plan a basket and explain what they leave out. Change one price and invite them to revise their choices. The objective is reasoning within a budget, not finding one perfect basket.

## Needs and wants sorting

Provide picture cards for food, shelter, a bus ticket, a game and a birthday gift. Ask children to explain why an item might change category depending on context. A bus ticket may be necessary for one person but optional for another. Avoid judging families' real purchases.

## The saving timeline

Draw four weekly boxes. A fictional character wants an £8 item and saves £2 per week. Children add coins or draw marks in each box. Ask what happens if the price rises to £10. For pupils who find writing difficult, use movable counters.

## Design a tiny business

Teams choose a pretend product, estimate a cost and set a price. Give them three customer requests and ask how they would respond. Explain that revenue is money received, while profit depends on costs. Avoid promising that every business idea will succeed.

## Spot the suspicious message

Show a fictional message asking someone to click an urgent payment link. Pupils circle pressure words, identify missing evidence and practise asking a trusted adult before acting. Do not use real account details or live links.

## Reflection and assessment

Finish with one question: 'What did you choose, and why?' Allow spoken, written, picture or AAC responses. A successful activity should reveal the child's reasoning. Reuse activities with different prices rather than adding complexity every week.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
