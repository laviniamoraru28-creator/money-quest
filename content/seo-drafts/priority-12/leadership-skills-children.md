# How to Teach Leadership Skills to Children

> Teach leadership through listening, fair decisions and shared responsibility rather than titles or popularity.

## Give everyone a meaningful role

In a small group project, rotate roles such as organiser, question-asker, materials checker and presenter. Allow non-speaking or AAC-based participation. Explain that a leader is responsible for the group's progress, not entitled to control others.

## Practise listening and disagreement

Present a fictional disagreement about how to use a shared £10 activity budget. Ask each child to summarise another person's view before proposing a decision. Model respectful disagreement and invite quieter members to contribute without pressure.

## Make decisions transparently

Ask the group to choose criteria, such as cost, fairness and safety. Compare options against those criteria. If the plan changes, explain why. This turns leadership into observable behaviours.

## Respond to mistakes

Use a scenario in which the group runs out of materials. Ask what happened, what can be repaired and how to prevent it next time. Avoid blaming one child for a group problem.

## Assess what matters

Look for inviting contributions, sharing responsibility, explaining choices and responding to feedback. Do not score confidence, volume or eye contact as leadership ability.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
