# How to Teach Children the Difference Between Needs and Wants

> Help children explain context, priorities and trade-offs without labelling ordinary pleasures as wrong.

## Use context, not rigid labels

Food is generally a need, but a particular restaurant meal may be a want. Transport can be essential for school or work. Ask 'Needed for what?' rather than treating every item as permanently one category.

## Try a three-column activity

Use picture cards and columns labelled 'Need now', 'Want now' and 'Depends'. Ask children to place each card and explain their choice. The 'Depends' column matters because it invites reasoning rather than guessing the adult's answer.

## Make choices with a fictional budget

A character has £7. They need a £4 lunch and would like a £5 toy. Ask what choices remain and why. For older children, introduce a free alternative or the possibility of saving over two weeks.

## Support different communication styles

Offer large images, limited choices, spoken instructions and an AAC option such as 'need', 'want', 'depends' and 'because'. Allow pointing, sorting or selecting. Do not require eye contact or lengthy speech as evidence of understanding.

## Avoid shame

Children do not control household resources. Do not invite pupils to disclose what their family can afford. The lesson is about reasoning and priorities, not whether a family makes 'good' or 'bad' purchases.

## Try this next

Choose one activity from this guide and repeat it with a different fictional example. Ask the child to explain a choice, show it with pictures or communicate it using their preferred method. Keep real family financial information private.
