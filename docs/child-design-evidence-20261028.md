# Evidence-informed refinement, 28 October 2026

These are design inferences, not evidence that Money Quest has been validated with children. The approved cinematic 3D style is retained; none of these studies establishes that 3D cartoon art itself is superior to other styles.

## Reading and activity progression

Explorer (6–8): concrete objects, small amounts, short instructions, pointing/drawing/AAC options. The original Explorer book uses one short paragraph per reading page. Shared reading remains welcome.

Builder (9–11): explain a choice, compare a small number of options and use an example. Strategist (12–14): distinguish observation from inference, compare evidence, identify costs/trade-offs, acknowledge uncertainty and limitations. These are authored difficulty targets, not a clinical assessment of age or ability; any child may choose a different level.

All 22 lab tasks now have different ideas, scenarios, answer wording and feedback at each level. Age-specific practical activities are preserved. The test checks distinct wording, but a difference in strings alone cannot establish educational quality. Editorial and child testing are still required.

## Visual and interaction decisions

- A phone-shaped Investing Lab is a simulation wrapper around the existing deterministic fictional prices. Its labels must continue to say pretend money. A real brokerage connection, pressure to trade, and profit promises are outside scope.
- The illustrated book spread retains a legible mobile layout rather than shrinking two desktop pages onto a phone. Contents and page buttons support autonomous navigation. Any page transition respects Reduced Motion.
- Night view uses black surfaces and white text. It is a user preference, not a claim of medical benefit or a universal reading advantage.
- The site-wide Listen button supplements local readers. Long text is chunked without dropping words. Voice availability is browser/OS dependent and actual playback needs device testing.

## Evaluation still needed

Observe children and grown-ups completing concrete tasks: find an activity, understand a payment tool, choose a level, turn a book page and recover from a wrong answer. Include different reading abilities, sensory needs, nonverbal/AAC participation and assistive technologies. Do not publish outcome claims based on automated accessibility checks, word counts or these design inferences alone.
