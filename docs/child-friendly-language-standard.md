# Money Quest: child-friendly language standard

Status: editorial guidance for review. This does not override factual accuracy, safeguarding or existing game work.

## Voice
- Warm, playful, respectful. Talk *with* children, not down to them.
- One idea at a time. Prefer 1–3 short sentences per instruction.
- Start with a concrete example or curious question. Explain unfamiliar words immediately.
- Use UK English and familiar examples: coins, shops, toys, friends, buses, school.
- Humour is welcome when it clarifies an idea; never joke about a child's mistake.
- Avoid unnecessary abstract words, unexplained abbreviations and moralising.
- Never imply all families have spare money, a bank card, or the same home life.
- Make choices meaningful. Avoid timers or competitive pressure as the default.

## Reading tiers
- Explorer: aim for 4–10 words per sentence, one task per screen; optional image/audio response.
- Builder: aim for 6–14 words per sentence, concrete examples and a short why.
- Strategist: aim for 8–18 words per sentence, introduce nuance without jargon.
These are editorial targets, not automatic pass/fail rules or developmental diagnoses.

## Inclusive writing
- Say what to do, not only what to avoid.
- Use short labels with a verb: "Listen", "Choose", "Try again", "Show me".
- Give feedback that helps: "Not quite. Look at the price again."
- Do not require reading aloud, speaking, speed or perfect fine-motor control.
- Support choice boards, text enlargement, audio, reduced motion and a quiet mode where technically feasible.
- Avoid describing a child as "low functioning" or assuming ability from a diagnosis.

## Examples
Before: "Evaluate the reliability of online information before reaching conclusions."
After: "Is it true? Be a fact detective. Check another source!"

Before: "Delayed gratification facilitates long-term financial goal attainment."
After: "Want that bike? Save a little at a time. Watch your goal get closer!"

Before: "Incorrect response."
After: "Not quite. Let's try another way."

## Editorial acceptance checklist
- Is the idea accurate?
- Would a child understand it without an adult translating?
- Is the action clear and accessible?
- Is the joke optional rather than necessary to understand?
- Does the text avoid pressure, shame and stereotypes?
- Is the text appropriately different for Explorer, Builder and Strategist?
- Are translations natural, rather than word-for-word copies?
