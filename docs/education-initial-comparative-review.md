# Initial human-readable curriculum comparison: Money Basics and Saving

Source: `messages/en.json` (English translation records, inspected 2026-11-20). This is a source-based editorial review, **not** a measured child outcome, diagnosis or verified runtime defect.

## Money Basics, three levels
- Explorer `/curriculum/explorer-money_basics-l1`: objective to recognise money as a way to obtain needs/wants; sort pictures into money/not-money; quiz identifies a coin. This is a concrete recognition task.
- Builder `/curriculum/builder-money_basics-l1`: objective to explain earning and cash/card/digital forms; match ways to earn to scenario cards; quiz identifies doing a job. This introduces earning but the sample quiz assesses only one part of the broader objective.
- Strategist `/curriculum/strategist-money_basics-l1`: objective about trust and different currencies; compare currencies; quiz asks why a shop might refuse a foreign banknote. The quiz is aligned with currency acceptance but does not directly assess the stated trust concept.

**Review candidate:** Check assessment coverage against each objective. For Builder, add or verify a question about digital balances versus cash. For Strategist, add or verify an application involving why people accept currency. These are proposals, not instructions to add redundant questions before inspecting the running experience.

## Saving, three levels
- Explorer `/curriculum/explorer-saving-l1`: recognise saving as keeping money for later; activity adds coins to a piggy bank; quiz asks for the definition.
- Builder `/curriculum/builder-saving-l1`: set a savings goal and consider immediate spending; activity tracks a target amount; quiz chooses saving over several weeks.
- Strategist `/curriculum/strategist-saving-l1`: compare saving choices and the effect of interest over time; activity compares scenarios; quiz asks why interest can matter more over time.

**Review candidate:** The three objectives and activities differ substantively in their English descriptions. Still inspect whether the actual interface implements goal tracking and scenario comparison, whether numerical assumptions are clear, and whether Explorer has non-reading support. The Strategist explanation should avoid implying interest always increases savings or that longer holding is universally preferable; actual account terms, fees and inflation matter.

## Cross-cutting questions for qualified reviewer
1. Are Explorer sorting and piggy-bank activities playable without reading, with narration and accessible alternatives?
2. Does feedback explain a choice, or only reward a correct click?
3. Can children transfer concepts to a new context, rather than repeat the example?
4. Do Builder and Strategist tasks require distinct reasoning in the rendered experience?
5. Does the financial content distinguish simplified teaching examples from general rules?

No curriculum files were changed. No claim about usability, reading ability, motivation or cognitive load is established without UI inspection and learner testing.
